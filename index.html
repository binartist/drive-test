Chapter 1

Drive on the left

Sit on the right, keep left, think in km/h, and leave extra time on winding roads.

Remember

Drive on the left. Keep left except when overtaking. A red light means stop — there is no free turn on red in New Zealand.

Do this every time

  • Sit on the right-hand side of the car
  • Keep left unless overtaking or turning right
  • Read speed signs in kilometres per hour
  • Seatbelts for everyone; phone must be hands-free
  • Slow early for bends, crests, and gravel

Chapter 2

Roundabouts

Give way to traffic from your right, travel clockwise, and always signal left to exit.

Signals that work in NZ

First exit (left)

Indicate left on approach, then left again as you exit.

Straight through

No signal on approach. Indicate left after you pass the exit before yours.

Right / more than halfway

Indicate right on approach, then left to leave.

Chapter 3

Who gives way

Uncontrolled intersections and T-junctions are where visitors get caught out.

Uncontrolled crossroads

Give way to traffic coming from your right.

T-intersection

If your road ends, you give way to the through road.

No turn on red

A red light means stop. Wait for green (or a green arrow).

Chapter 4

Parking

Yellow lines mean different things. Always read the pole sign.

Yellow lines (NZ)

Solid yellow = no stopping (not even briefly). Broken yellow = no parking (brief passenger drop-off only if you stay with the car).

  • Read the pole sign for zone, time limit, and fees
  • Never block driveways or bus stops
  • In Auckland paid areas: pay by plate at the machine or use the AT Park app

Chapter 5

Filling petrol

It’s called petrol here. Most rentals take 91. Match the flap sticker.

  1. Park with the flap toward the pump and switch the engine off
  2. Check the flap sticker: usually 91, sometimes 95/98, or diesel
  3. Lift the matching nozzle, insert fully, squeeze — it auto-stops when full
  4. Hang the nozzle up and close the flap
  5. Pay at the pump or inside (remember your pump number). Some night sites ask you to prepay first
Never mix fuels

Petrol in a diesel car (or the reverse) can wreck the engine. If unsure, ask staff before you lift a nozzle.
